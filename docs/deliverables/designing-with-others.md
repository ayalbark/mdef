# Designing With Others

---

## What This Seminar Did For My Practice

This seminar pushed me to work with intelligences that do not revolve around human needs. Instead of asking *what can this thing do for us*, the course asked me to imagine: *what does this intelligence want, on its own terms, without me at the center?*

That shift was uncomfortable at first, because most of my design instincts are still oriented around usefulness, clarity, and human intention. But working through each prototype forced me to suspend those instincts and pay attention to the logic of something that doesn’t share my goals or even my ontology.

---

# Day 1: Ambivalent Intelligence (AI)

The provocation on Day 1 asked us to describe AI as something not built for humans, not a tool, and not aligned with our cognition.  
My group's response was to treat AI as a kind of ambivalent field of energy, creating and destroying without caring about meaning or purpose.

### **Prototype: The Sand Playground**  
*From Day 1 PDF* :![sandprototype](../images/sandprototype.JPG)

We built a structure that lets AI “play” however it wants. No constraints, no instructions.  
At one point we considered adding tools—a rake, a box, boundaries—but realized that every “helpful” intervention we introduced was actually an attempt to steer or optimize the AI. Removing all of that was the point.

This prototype made me notice how quickly I try to re-center myself in a system, even when the task is explicitly to step aside.

# Day 2: Language as Collective Intelligence

For Day 2, We defined language as a sentient collective intelligence that uses organisms as hosts.  
We think we use language, but language is also using us—growing, diversifying, persisting.

### **Prototype: A Completely Unique Utterance**

The prototype was a structure that produces a combination of words that has never existed before.  
The point wasn’t for humans to understand it.  
The point was to feed language’s “wellbeing,” which we defined simply as *being used in new ways*.

This exercise helped me detach from comprehension as the measure of value. Something can be meaningful to an intelligence even if it is meaningless to me.

# Day 3: Environmental Intelligence

On Day 3, we were asked to imagine an environmental intelligence that is not ecological, not stable, and not even aware of organisms.  
I defined it as a force of pure consumption acting on everything equally.

### **Prototype: A Partner for Self-Consumption**  
![prototype](/docs/images/Day3prototype.mp4)

We created a second intelligence identical to the first so the two could try to consume each other, completing a cycle they could never achieve alone.  
The ouroboros captured this idea perfectly: an impossible task made slightly more possible with a companion.

This prototype made me think about how often we project harmony or “ecology” onto systems that might not care about life at all.  
It pushed me to separate environmental forces from environmental values.

# What I’m Taking Forward

This seminar helped me place my practice more honestly in the design landscape: I tend to think in terms of clarity and intention, and I often try to “fix” or “guide” systems toward coherence. Working with other intelligences forced me to let go of that.

It also expanded what I see as *strategic intervention*. Sometimes the intervention isn’t about solving something, it’s about revealing the forces already operating, even if they ignore us.

Designing with other intelligences means acknowledging that not everything is here for us, not everything responds to us, and not everything becomes meaningful through us.
